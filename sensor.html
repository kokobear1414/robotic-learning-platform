General Sensors in Robotics

Explore the main types of sensors used in robotics, and understand how they help robots sense, decide, and act in the real world.

Introduction

Robots rely on sensors to detect their environment and interact with it safely. Sensors are the “eyes” and “ears” of robots, helping them navigate, avoid obstacles, measure environmental conditions, and perform tasks autonomously.

Types of Sensors

  • Proximity Sensors: Detect obstacles or walls near the robot.
  • Light Sensors: Measure brightness to adjust cameras or follow lines.
  • Temperature Sensors: Monitor environment or robot overheating.
  • IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit): Measures orientation, tilt, and acceleration for balancing and motion control.
  • Camera: Captures images for object detection and navigation.
  • Gas Sensors: Detect hazardous gases for safety in industrial robots.

How Sensors Help Robots

In robotics, sensors are used to:

  • Detect obstacles and avoid collisions
  • Navigate using maps and landmarks
  • Monitor the robot's internal state (temperature, battery)
  • Interact with objects using robotic arms or grippers
  • Perform environmental monitoring tasks like gas detection or light measurement
Combining multiple sensors improves reliability. For example, a robot can combine proximity sensors and cameras to navigate accurately even if one sensor fails.

Sensor Fusion Relation

While each sensor gives partial information, robots often use sensor fusion techniques to combine readings from multiple sensors into one accurate estimate of the environment. For example, IMU + GPS + Camera = better navigation for drones or autonomous cars.

  • Reduces noise and error from individual sensors
  • Enables robust decision-making
  • Improves localization, mapping, and motion planning
